Undergraduate thesis · Information Technology

Estimating the Right Price: Development of a Used Vehicle Price Prediction System using Online Marketplace Data

🎯 Problem

Buyers and sellers in the Philippine used-car market rely on guesswork and scattered listings to judge a fair price, leading to overpricing, underselling, and long negotiation cycles.

🧮 Approach

Listings were scraped and cleaned from four online marketplaces, then used to train a multiple linear regression model that maps vehicle attributes to an expected market price.

📊 Output

The system returns a point estimate, a confidence range, and a feature-by-feature breakdown so users understand why a car is priced the way it is — not just the number.

Why regression

Model choice and what it trades off.

Multiple linear regression was chosen for its interpretability: every coefficient can be read on its own as the peso effect of one feature, which matters for a tool meant to explain a price rather than just output one. Tree-based models (random forest, gradient boosting) were tested as a comparison baseline and improved raw accuracy slightly, but at the cost of the transparent, per-feature breakdown shown on the Predict Price page — a deliberate trade-off given the system's goal of building user trust in the estimate.
